# China's Cheap AI vs India's Sovereign AI: What It Means for Business

Published on July 30, 2026 • AI Geopolitics • Sovereign AI • LLMs

**In plain words**

Chinese AI companies such as DeepSeek and Alibaba now sell access to their models for a small fraction of what US providers charge. Part of that is clever engineering and part is government support. India is responding by building its own models for Indian languages and its own data centres. For a business owner, this means AI keeps getting cheaper and you have more choice about where your data is processed; for help picking and building on the right model, see [AI agent development](https://www.tensix.in/services/ai-agent-development).

## 1. Why Chinese AI Is So Cheap, and Can It Last?

Since early 2026, Chinese AI labs have started a global price war. They charge a small fraction of what leading US models cost per request (API inference: paying per piece of text processed). The aim is to become the default, low-cost AI engine for business automation worldwide.

### What the Prices Look Like

- **DeepSeek:** A major catalyst in the price war, with models like DeepSeek V4-Flash reaching as low as **$0.14 per 1M input tokens**. DeepSeek aggressively utilizes cache-hit pricing, saving developers massive amounts on repetitive workloads.
- **Alibaba (Qwen):** On Alibaba Cloud's Model Studio, Qwen3.5 Flash is priced at near-zero rates: **~$0.10 per 1M input tokens**.

### Real Efficiency or Government Subsidy?

- **Genuine Efficiency (Valid):** Chinese labs prioritize *inference economics* over raw scaling. They use Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) designs, where only part of the model works on each request, so each answer needs much less computing power.
- **State-Subsidized "Dumping":** To offset the "inefficiency tax" of using domestic AI chips, local Chinese governments offer **electricity subsidies of up to 50%**. The national "East Data, West Computing" initiative directs compute to provinces rich in cheap renewables.
- **Conclusion:** The cheap prices are economically "real" for the end user, but sustained by a dual-engine: elite architectural efficiency paired with state-backed infrastructural subsidies.

## 2. India's Answer: Its Own Models and Data Centres

India does not want to depend fully on "renting" AI from a few US companies or on Chinese state-backed models. Its "Sovereign AI" push focuses on Indian data, models that understand Indian languages and culture, and large data centres inside the country.

### A. Indian AI Models

- **Krutrim:** Developed by Ola Krutrim AI Labs, this foundational LLM is trained on the largest known collection of Indic data.
- **Sarvam AI:** Backed by the government's IndiaAI Mission, Sarvam builds models from scratch (e.g., Sarvam-105B) optimized for Indian languages.
- **Jio Brain:** Reliance’s enterprise AI/ML platform integrating tightly with its 5G edge network.
- **Bhashini:** A DPI project providing open-source APIs for speech-to-text and translation.

### B. Indian Data Centres and Computing Power

- **Adani Group:** Committing **$100 billion by 2035**, Adani is building an "energy-compute" model, powering high-density AI infrastructure with massive solar/renewable grids.
- **Reliance Industries:** Committing roughly **$110 billion**, Reliance is building multi-gigawatt data centers in Jamnagar (including a hub leased to Meta).
- **Yotta Data Services:** Operating the **Shakti Cloud**, Yotta is scaling to over 85,000 NVIDIA GPUs, hosting the sovereign Government Community Cloud.

### C. Developers and Digital Public Infrastructure

- **Developer Boom:** India is currently #2 globally on **GitHub** (projected #1 by 2028) and constitutes roughly 10.4% of total traffic on **Hugging Face**. This grassroots army is shifting India from an IT services consumer to an open-source AI contributor.
- **DPI (Digital Public Infrastructure):** Just as India scaled Aadhaar and UPI, it is treating foundational AI models as digital public goods. India is actively exporting this DPI framework to the Global South as a democratic alternative.
