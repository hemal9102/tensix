# Cold Outreach — 3 AI Companies (ready to send)

**Written for:** you, to send one-to-one from your own email. Each is personalized
to that company's real scan result. Do not mass-send; send individually, ideally
to a named person (founder / head of growth — find on their About page or LinkedIn).

**The sales logic underneath all three (so you can improvise on replies):**
- Lead with a *specific, true, verifiable* gap, not a pitch. Proof first.
- Name the wound in business terms (AI assistants skip/misread you), never RFC jargon.
- The irony hook — "you sell AI, but AI can't read your site" — is the emotional lever. Use it lightly; imply it, don't gloat.
- The ask is tiny and safe: a free teardown, then a fixed, small price. No guaranteed-outcome claims. Specificity *is* the credibility.
- Sign with the IETF draft. That's the one thing a random freelancer can't fake.

**Pricing in these:** $750 founding rate for the fix, free teardown as the hook.
Subject lines matter more than the body for opens — each has an A and B option.

## How to actually run these (the part that decides if it works)

1. **Find a real person.** Send to a named founder / head of growth, not
   `hello@`. Check their site's About page, then LinkedIn/X. A name in the
   greeting doubles reply rates.
2. **Send one at a time, from your normal email** (hemal@tensix.in). Not a
   bulk tool. Three hand-sent emails outperform 300 blasted ones and won't
   burn your domain.
3. **When someone replies "send it" — this is the real moment.** Actually run
   the full scan, write 1 page: the 3 gaps, what each costs them, the exact
   fix, the $750. That page closes the deal, not the email.
4. **Follow up once, 4–5 days later, one line:** "Did the teardown make sense —
   want me to do the fix?" Most replies come on the follow-up, not the first.
5. **Don't discount below $750.** If they hesitate on price, offer to do the
   single biggest gap first for less, not the whole thing cheaper. Anchor holds.

## What NOT to do (protects the close)
- No "guaranteed #1 in ChatGPT" — unprovable, reads as scam, kills trust.
- No RFC jargon in the first email. Business pain only.
- No attachment on the first email (triggers spam). Link only.
- Don't argue if they say "we don't think AI search matters yet." Reply:
  "Totally fair — want the free teardown anyway so you have it when it does?"
  Keeps the door open.
